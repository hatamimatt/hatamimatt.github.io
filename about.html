A bit about me

I am a PhD student in hydrology at the University of Florida, in the Climate Resilience Lab of Dr. Nasser Najibi’s research group. My research focuses on hydroclimate extremes and risk: characterizing and modeling floods, droughts, extreme precipitation and heatwaves, and developing stochastic decision frameworks that help infrastructure and ecosystems stay resilient under deep uncertainty.

I combine process-based and ensemble approaches, statistics, machine learning, remote sensing and geospatial tools to assess climate vulnerability across water, energy, transportation, agriculture and public health. Along the way I build the software that makes this analysis usable: web GIS applications, data pipelines and interactive tools like the globe on this site.

My path to this work started with river hydrology and hydraulics during my BSc in Iran, continued through disaster risk mitigation in Milan and cascading-disaster modeling in Groningen, and led to a PhD in water resources at the University of South Carolina before I moved to UF.

Research interests

Hydroclimate extremes, extreme precipitation, multi-hazard risk assessment, compound and cascading disasters, statistical analysis, water systems modeling, climate change adaptation, geospatial machine learning.
